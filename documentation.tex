%% ── 1. Overview ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Project Overview}

\begin{tcolorbox}[infobox,title=Declaration of Authorship]
I declare that this application --- \textbf{EconCalc} --- is my original and independent work.
The ECON mode (Cash Flow Analyzer) is an original feature not present in the physical
Casio fx-991ES PLUS calculator. All code was written by the author.
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{0.5em}
\textbf{EconCalc} is an Android scientific calculator application inspired by the Casio fx-991ES PLUS.
It replicates the full functionality of the physical calculator in a mobile app, adds an original
Engineering Economics analysis mode (ECON Mode), and includes a graphical equation solver.

\subsection{Motivation}
The physical Casio fx-991ES PLUS has key limitations in an Engineering Economics course:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item Cannot compute Net Present Value (NPV) from variable cash flows
  \item Cannot compute Future Worth (FW) or Annual Worth (AW)
  \item Requires manual lookup from printed Appendix A factor tables
  \item No visual representation of cash flow timelines
\end{itemize}
EconCalc solves all of these with its original \textbf{ECON Mode}.

%% ── 4. Original Feature ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Original Feature: ECON Mode --- Cash Flow Analyzer}

\begin{tcolorbox}[econbox,title={$\bigstar$\; Original Feature}]
\textbf{ECON Mode} is the original feature of EconCalc. It does not exist in the physical
Casio fx-991ES PLUS and directly addresses a limitation in the ENCE 356 course.
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{Mathematical Formulas}

The six standard TVM factors for interest rate $i$ and periods $n$:
\begin{align}
  P/F &= \frac{1}{(1+i)^n}, & F/P &= (1+i)^n, \\[4pt]
  P/A &= \frac{(1+i)^n-1}{i(1+i)^n}, & A/P &= \frac{i(1+i)^n}{(1+i)^n-1}, \\[4pt]
  F/A &= \frac{(1+i)^n-1}{i}, & A/F &= \frac{i}{(1+i)^n-1}.
\end{align}

Net Present Value from variable cash flows $C_t$:
\[
  \mathrm{NPV} = \sum_{t=0}^{n} \frac{C_t}{(1+i)^t}, \qquad
  \mathrm{FW} = \mathrm{NPV}\times(1+i)^n, \qquad
  \mathrm{AW} = \mathrm{NPV}\times(A/P,\,i,\,n).
\]

\subsection{Verification Against Textbook}
Test: Rs.~50,000 investment, Rs.~15,000/year inflow for 5 years, $i=10\%$.

\subsection{ECON Mode Features}
\begin{tcolorbox}[featurebox,title=What ECON Mode provides]
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,nosep]
  \item Variable cash flow entry for $t=0$ through $t=n$ (inflows and outflows)
  \item NPV, FW, and AW computed with 4-decimal precision
  \item Full factor table for all 6 TVM factors
  \item Visual cash flow timeline diagram (scrollable)
  \item Automatic \textbf{ACCEPT / REJECT} decision based on NPV sign
  \item Replaces Appendix A printed factor tables from ENCE 356 textbook
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}

%% ── 5. Graph Feature ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Graph Feature}

EconCalc includes an SVG-based graphing engine (\texttt{react-native-svg}) that activates
automatically in two modes:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{EQN Mode} --- plots the curve after solving, with orange dots on real roots.
        Uses bisection scan across 2,000 intervals to guarantee all roots are found.
  \item \textbf{TABLE Mode} --- sparkline graph shown below the $f(x)$ numerical table.
\end{itemize}
Features: auto-scaling axes, gridlines, smart tick labels, discontinuity detection.

%% ── 6. Statistical Distributions ────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Statistical Distributions (DIST Mode)}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Normal CDF} (Abramowitz \& Stegun 7.1.26, error $<1.5\times10^{-7}$):
  \[P(X\leq x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\!\left[1+\mathrm{erf}\!\left(\tfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma\sqrt{2}}\right)\right]\]
  \item \textbf{Binomial CDF} (log-factorial to avoid overflow):
  \[P(X\leq k)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n}{i}p^i(1-p)^{n-i}\]
  \item \textbf{Poisson CDF} (running term update):
  \[P(X\leq k)=e^{-\lambda}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{\lambda^i}{i!}\]
\end{itemize}
Note: \texttt{Math.erf} is absent from JavaScript. A custom polynomial approximation
was implemented and verified against standard z-tables.
